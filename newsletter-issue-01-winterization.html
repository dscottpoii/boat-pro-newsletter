The Ultimate Boat Winterization Guide

Properly winterizing your boat is the most critical step you can take to protect your investment and ensure a trouble-free launch next spring. Freeze damage to an engine block or plumbing system can be catastrophic and expensive — this issue covers the full checklist.

Welcome aboard. This is a full, field-tested guide — not a teaser. Read it, print it, and check off every box before your boat goes on the hard.

Before you start: this is a general guide. Always consult your specific engine and boat owner's manuals for procedures, fluids, and specs unique to your vessel.

Section 1 — Engine & Fuel System

This is the most critical part of the process. Water left in the engine block or cooling system will freeze, expand, and can crack the block or manifold.

Inboard & Sterndrive Engines

  1. Warm the engine using muffs or a fake-a-lake for 5–10 minutes to open the thermostat.
  2. Change oil & filter while warm — old, acidic oil damages internals during storage.
  3. Flush the cooling system with fresh water to clear salt, silt, and mineral deposits.
  4. Circulate antifreeze — the recommended method is drawing non-toxic pink marine antifreeze through the raw-water intake until it runs pink at the exhaust ports. Shut down immediately; never run the bucket dry.

Outboard Engines

  1. Flush with muffs and a garden hose, 5–10 minutes.
  2. Tilt the engine fully down so it self-drains completely.
  3. Change gearcase oil — milky or creamy oil means a seal needs repair before storage.
  4. Change 4-stroke oil and filter if applicable.

All Engine Types

  • Fill the fuel tank to 90–95% and add a quality marine fuel stabilizer.
  • Run the engine 10–15 minutes to circulate stabilized fuel through lines, filters, and injectors.
  • Fog the engine — spray fogging oil into the intake until the engine stalls, coating cylinders against rust.
  • Replace the fuel/water separator and any inline fuel filters.
Field note

The single most common mistake we see at the yard: draining instead of flushing antifreeze, and missing a petcock. Flushing until pink antifreeze runs from the exhaust is the safer, more complete method — it protects manifolds and risers that draining alone often misses.

Section 2 — Plumbing & Freshwater Systems

Any water left in your potable water system will freeze and crack lines, pumps, and fixtures.

  1. Drain the freshwater tank completely by opening every faucet and running the pump dry.
  2. Bypass the hot water heater — never fill it with antifreeze. Set bypass valves, then drain it fully.
  3. Disconnect the pump's intake from the tank and draw pink RV/marine antifreeze through it instead.
  4. Run every faucet, shower, and washdown — hot and cold — until pure pink antifreeze flows out.
  5. Pump out the holding tank, then add antifreeze and flush it through the toilet system to protect the pump and macerator.

Section 3 — Interior & Exterior

  • Clean the hull, deck, and topsides thoroughly, then apply a protective coat of wax.
  • Remove all food and trash; empty the fridge/freezer and prop the doors open.
  • Remove all fabrics — cushions, linens, PFDs, curtains — this is the #1 way to prevent a musty cabin in spring.
  • Prop open interior doors, lockers, and drawers to promote airflow.
  • Place moisture absorbers or a boat-safe dehumidifier inside the cabin.

Section 4 — Battery & Electrical

  • Fully charge, then remove batteries; clean terminals with a wire brush.
  • Store at home in a cool, dry place — never directly on concrete — on a smart battery maintainer.
  • Consider removing expensive electronics like chartplotters or VHF radios.

Section 5 — Cover & Storage

  • Cover with shrink-wrap (most effective) or a high-quality custom canvas cover.
  • Support the cover with a frame so snow and water run off instead of pooling.
  • Ventilate — trapped moisture is the #1 cause of mildew, even under a good cover.
  • Set mouse traps or non-toxic pest repellents before you close it up for the season.
